\chapter{Running \banzai{} from the raw frames}
\label{sec:raw}

The corrections of Chapters~\ref{sec:traces}--\ref{sec:flux} were first developed on the archive products, undoing
parts of the archive processing where necessary (improved v1, Appendix~\ref{sec:app_v1}). Running the pipeline from the
raw frames removes the need to undo anything. The pixels destroyed in \banzai{}~1.0.1 by the division by a shifted
lamp flat (Chapter~\ref{sec:fringe}) are never created. The 2-d frame is resampled only once, onto the corrected
wavelength grid. The uncertainties are propagated from the read noise and the photon counts. Standards and science
exposures are processed in the same way. Running from raw also gives the collaboration a reproducible pipeline for
new data. This chapter describes how we ran \banzai{} on our own computer, the run of \banzai{}~1.1.0 with its
default settings (`stock') that serves as the baseline, and the comparison of the five reductions
(Table~\ref{tab:versions_names}; see also the Conventions, p.~\pageref{sec:conventions}).

\section{Data and calibration database}
\label{sec:raw_data}
The raw data set (Table~\ref{tab:rawdata}) contains 100 Fairall~9 exposures in 50 blocks of two consecutive
exposures on 47 nights (2026 August 28 -- November 11); every block has its own arc and lamp flat. The 57 standard-star
exposures are all through the 2\,arcsec slit: 39 of Feige\,110, which is in the \banzai{} catalogue of flux standards
and has a finely sampled reference spectrum, and 18 of EG\,21. Forty of the 47 Fairall~9 nights have a standard
(Feige\,110 on 25, EG\,21 on 15). The nearest standard was observed a median of 1.5\,h from the science block, and
within 3\,h for 33 of the 50 blocks.

We ran \banzai{}~1.1.0, newer than the 1.0.1 that produced the archive, on a laptop without access to the LCO
servers. Instead of the LCO calibration database it uses a local database (an SQLite file). Our set-up scripts (in
\texttt{work/banzai\_run}) create the sites and instruments in this database and load the files distributed with the
package: the order heights and locations, the line-spread-function parameters and the flux standards. They then
process the four sky flats, whose observation type we set to \texttt{SKYFLAT}, as the calibration notebook distributed
with the package does. The order traces fitted on these sky flats reproduce the archive order traces (header
\texttt{ORDER\_COEFFS}) to $\leq0.7$\,px; we attribute the difference to the changes to the order fitting between 1.0.1
and 1.1.0. The frames are then reduced in this sequence: arcs, lamp flats, standards and science, so that every frame
finds its calibrations already in the database. EG\,21 is not in the catalogue of flux standards, so it is reduced as
a science frame.

\section{Uncertainties}
\label{sec:raw_errors}
We tested the uncertainties on the 24 pairs of Fairall~9 exposures taken in the same block. The AGN does not vary
within a block, and both exposures share the same arc, so the wavelength bins of the two extracted spectra match one
to one. We scaled the second spectrum to the first by their ratio smoothed over 300\,\AA{} and computed the pull
$(f_1-f_2)/\sqrt{\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2}$ in each bin. If the uncertainties are right, its rms is 1
(Fig.~\ref{fig:errors}).

In the 2-d frames the uncertainty of a pixel is $\sqrt{N+R^2}$ electrons, with $N$ the counts in electrons and $R$ the
read noise. On the sky, the pull between the same pixel in the two exposures has an rms of 1.05. The error of the
extracted spectrum of improved v3 agrees to 1--2 per cent with the optimal-extraction error \citep{Horne1986}
recomputed from the 2-d pixels. For the extracted spectra, the pull rms of improved v3 is 0.89--0.93 in the blue order
and 0.92--1.05 in the red. For comparison, \banzai{}~1.1.0 reaches up to 1.6 in the red order, and \banzai{}~1.0.1
reaches 1.7--2.2 beyond 7700\,\AA, where its errors do not include the fringe residuals.

These pulls are computed at each wavelength across the 24 pairs. Computed instead along the wavelength axis of each
pair, which also captures differences between the exposures on scales of a few pixels, the pull rms is 1.09--1.12,
rising to 1.2--1.4 in the fringe and telluric regions. Summing 4--30 adjacent pixels raises it to $\approx1.2$--1.3.
This excess comes from fringe and telluric residuals and small wavelength shifts, which no error array contains. For
integrated line fluxes, a systematic term of this size should therefore be added to the pipeline errors, or the
scatter between exposures used instead.
